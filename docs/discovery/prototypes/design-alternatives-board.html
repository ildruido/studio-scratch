Three directions for the booking page

Three different ideas for how a student books a class at a one-room yoga studio. They differ in what the page is organised around, not in colour. Sketches use made-up sample data.

A board is not evidence of demand. Nothing here was shown to a studio owner or a student, and Maya's booking pain is still unproven (R-001). Anything marked "later" is an idea, not scope.

DIRECTION A

The week at a glance

The page is the studio's timetable. A student sees the whole week as a grid, taps a class, books. Familiar and fast, and closest to what most booking tools already do.

Weaknesses

  • Nothing ties it to a one-room studio
Build effortSmall
DIRECTION B

Just tell me the next class

No timetable up front. The page asks one thing ("When do you want to practise?") and offers the single best class, with one tap to book and two alternatives underneath. Built for the regular who just wants "tomorrow morning".

Open link→Pick a time of day→See one suggested class→One tap to book→Confirmation

Strengths

  • Fewest taps for returning students
  • Feels personal, not like a table
  • Phone-first by design

Weaknesses

  • Hides the full schedule (a new student may want to browse)
  • Needs remembering who you are, which brings in sign-in questions
  • Suggestion logic can feel wrong
Build effortMedium
DIRECTION C

Pick your mat

The page is the room itself. A one-room studio has a fixed floor with maybe twelve mats, so the student picks a class, then taps the exact spot they want (by the window, at the back, near the door). Booking becomes choosing where you'll be, which a big studio tool cannot offer.

Open link→Pick a day→Pick a class→Tap a mat on the floor plan→Name + email→Confirmation with your spot

Strengths

  • Only works for a one-room studio, so it is the product's own identity
  • Gives regulars a reason to come back to the page
  • Solves a real small-studio question: who stands where

Weaknesses

  • Owner must set up the floor plan once
  • Studios with free-for-all mats may not want it
  • More to build than a grid
Build effortMedium to large

Recommendation: C, with ideas from A and B

C is the only one that could not be mistaken for any booking tool, which is what the "generic" complaint is about. Take from B the "your usual" shortcut for returning students. Take from A a simple week strip at the top, so people can still browse. Park the owner's floor-plan editor as a later idea: in the first version the room could be one fixed layout.

The risk: it rests on the guess that owners and students care about choosing a spot. A quick question to a few owners would check that before building it.
