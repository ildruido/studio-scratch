# R-001 Do one-room yoga studio owners struggle with bookings: report

Protocol: `protocol.md`, pre-registered in PR #80, on `main` as commit fb4741f since 2026-11-26. Tier: standard. Method: `.claude/packs/research/FRAMEWORK.md`.

## Verdict

**Needs primary research.** Desk research found no evidence that bookings specifically are a top problem for one-room yoga studio owners, and no evidence that they are not. What the opened sources do show is a strained independent-studio business: admin overload, rising costs and competition. Only one search round was run, with Reddit unreachable, so the evidence is thin and every verdict is Low confidence. No kill criterion was triggered, though U3 is near one (see Findings). No verdict rests on sources labelled "accepted, not opened".

## Strongest evidence against our idea

The one opened first-hand owner account says administration, people management and marketing took about 90 percent of her time, and booking is not named (E-04). The second ex-owner essay lists profitability, not booking (E-05). The press names rising costs and closures (E-06, E-07). Booking software is a crowded category and Mindbody rates about 4 of 5 across thousands of Capterra reviews (E-10). A plausible reading is that owners hurt for money and students and booking is a solved, minor annoyance. The evidence neither shows nor rules that out.

## Findings

- **U1.** IBISWorld's free pages, opened, count Pilates and yoga studios together: 37,377 in the US, 5,011 in the UK and 3,823 in Australia, about 46,200 across three markets (Canada not found). Yoga-only and independent shares are unknown, so the 10,000 threshold cannot be checked, but it is plausible. The US count was reported as declining over 2021 to 2026. Leans supported, Low.
- **U2.** One owner says she did the admin herself (E-04). The big-studio podcast (E-12) shows larger studios using staff and software, but is a look-alike. Insufficient.
- **U3.** The three-community minimum is not met: only two owner accounts were opened (E-04, E-05), both former owners writing about closing. Neither names booking, and neither ranks it low, they simply do not mention it. The kill criterion needs "ranks low in at least 2 independent communities", so I read it as not triggered, but it is a warning sign. Vendor pages say booking hurts; they carry no weight. Insufficient.
- **U4.** Capterra's Mindbody reviews (E-10) mix praise and complaints (price rises, fees of "$500 or more a month"), from many roles outside our population and many before 2021, and are one platform. Insufficient.
- **Biggest problems in their own words.** Of what was opened: profitability and costs, administrative load, competition. Booking was not named.

## What we could not find

Any Reddit or forum thread (refused). Owner comments on booking, no-shows or double bookings that I opened. Yoga-only or independent studio counts. Canadian counts. Owner podcasts and Facebook groups were not searched. None of this means the problem is absent.

## Who is missing and other limits

Quiet owners, owners who use staff, owners content with free tools, and current owners (the opened voices are people who closed or wrote essays). E-05 is undated and probably before 2021, so it is context only. Geography is mostly US and Australia in the press, US for the podcast.

## What would change our mind

A second round with owner podcasts or forum access, or about ten owner interviews, in which several owners unprompted rank booking or no-shows near the top (U3 to Supported), or say they are content with a free tool (U3 and U4 to Contradicted). An opened source putting independent yoga studios under 10,000 in the four markets would trigger U1.

## Evidence-backed traits, segment candidates, look-alikes

- **Traits (all Low confidence):** the opened accounts come from owner-teachers who do the admin themselves and report overload. No variation by tools, budget or country can be claimed.
- **Segment candidates:** none supported yet. Suggested to test: solo owner-teachers who run everything; studios with a part-time admin.
- **Look-alikes:** studios with 75 or more teachers on Mindbody (E-12); personal trainers and other Capterra reviewers.

## Next: primary research needed

1. A second desk round is cheapest: owner podcasts (e.g. on Buzzsprout), Capterra switcher reviews and anything with Reddit or Facebook-group access. It could move U3 and U4 off Insufficient.
2. Eight to ten short interviews with one-room studio owners: how they take bookings, what went wrong last month, what they pay. This is the real test of U3 to U6.
3. A fake-door test, a one-page "booking page for your studio" with a sign-up button.
